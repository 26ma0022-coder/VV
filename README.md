# Vanishing Voices of Manbhum

A small digital archive of seven words from the Manbhum borderland (the region around modern Dhanbad, Purulia and Bokaro) that fell out of use when laws, borders and industries changed. Every word is traced to a source.

Built as a team project for the Digital Humanities course, Department of Humanities and Social Sciences, IIT (ISM) Dhanbad, 2026.

## The seven words

| Word | Domain | Era |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mānbhūm | Territorial / administrative geography | more than 200 years |
| Ghāṭwālī | Feudal defence / land tenure | more than 200 years |
| Chākarān | Feudal tenure / rural policing | more than 200 years |
| Panchak | Agrarian law / land revenue | more than 200 years |
| Kamīn | Colliery labour / gendered occupational argot | more than 100 years |
| Kamiya | Agrarian law / unfree labour | more than 100 years |
| Dhowrah | Industrial settlement / company housing | more than 50 years |

Each entry gives a definition, a usage span, a geographic scope, the cause of obsolescence and its provenance. Words are grouped into three eras: feudal and pre-colonial (more than 200 years), early industrial (more than 100 years), and late colonial to nationalisation (more than 50 years).

## How the words were found

1. The 1911 *Bengal District Gazetteers: Manbhum* (Coupland) was taken from archive.org as plain text and analysed with Voyant Tools. Recurring terms that are unfamiliar today stood out, for example *ghatwal\** (166 occurrences), *chakran* (12) and *panchak\** (10).
2. Candidate words were traced in further sources: the 1935 *Calcutta Gazette* and the 1952 *Industrial Housing in India* for *dhowrah*, Lahiri-Dutt (2006) for *kamin*, and a study of bonded labour in India for *kamiya*. A legal dictionary was used for *chakran* and *panchak*.
3. The 1877 *Statistical Account of Bengal* (Vol. 17) and the 1905 *Linguistic Survey of India* (Vol. V, Part II) were used for regional and linguistic context.
4. The administrative history was traced back about 200 years to understand what Dhanbad was part of before Jharkhand. This made the name "Manbhum" the most useful search term.

## About the name Manbhum

"Manbhum" was a local name before administrations formalised it as a district in 1833. Its origin is not known for certain. Two accounts are commonly given, and neither is confirmed here:

- It derives from Man Singh.
- It reflects a land that was respected (माननीय).

These are noted as open questions, not as findings. They are deliberately left out of the website.

## Not included

- *Korkar* and *khandit* appeared only once each in the 1911 Gazetteer, which is not enough to document them.
- Three words were heard orally from a retired colliery foreman: *khol* (mine entry), *mainer* (miner) and *kamini* (earned money from a job). They have no written reference yet, so they are not archived.

## Features

- Filter the entries by era
- A permanent link and a copyable citation for each entry (the citation includes the entry's URL and the date of access)
- A timeline showing which law or change retired each word
- Light and dark themes, and a layout that works on phones

## Citing an entry

Use the "Copy citation" button on the entry. The format is:
