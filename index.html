Vanishing Voices of Manbhum

Seven words from the Manbhum borderland, the region around modern Dhanbad, Purulia and Bokaro, that fell out of use when laws, borders and industries changed. Each word is traced to a source.

7 words from 6 sources in 3 eras.

মানভূম / मानभूम (Mānbhūm)

>200 yr Old Territorial / Administrative Geography
  • Definition: The historical regional designation encompassing modern Dhanbad, Purulia, and parts of Bokaro; serving as the dialectal transition zone between western Bengali and eastern Hindi/Magahi varieties.
  • Active Usage Span: Late 18th Century – 1956
  • Geographic Scope: Manbhum District (Jungle Mahals → Bengal Presidency → Bihar → split 1956).
  • Cause of Obsolescence: Administrative & Cartographic Reorganization. Dissolved under the States Reorganisation Act (1956), bifurcating the territory between Purulia (West Bengal) and Dhanbad (Bihar, now Jharkhand).
  • Archival Provenance:
    1. H. Coupland, Bengal District Gazetteers: Manbhum (1911), Ch. II: History. [Archive.org Link]

ঘাটওয়ালী / घाटवाली (Ghāṭwālī)

>200 yr Old Feudal Defense / Land Tenure
  • Definition: A quasi-military system where local indigenous chieftains and wardens (Ghatwals) were granted hereditary rent-free or low-quit-rent land rights in exchange for guarding strategic hill passes (ghats) against invaders and bandits.
  • Active Usage Span: Pre-Colonial (~1765) – 1950
  • Geographic Scope: Chota Nagpur & Manbhum border passes (Bengal Presidency).
  • Cause of Obsolescence: Statutory Abolition of Service Lands. Made obsolete by modern police administrations and formally dissolved by the Bihar Land Reforms Act of 1950.
  • Archival Provenance:
    1. H. Coupland, Bengal District Gazetteers: Manbhum (1911), Ch. XI: Land Revenue Administration ("Police Tenures in Manbhum"). [Archive.org Link]

धौड़ा / Dhowrah (Dhaura)

>50 yr Old Industrial Settlement / Company Housing
  • Definition: Standardized, compact tenement lines built by private coal companies under colonial municipal guidelines to house migrant colliery labor, superseding the concept of an ancestral village (basti).
  • Active Usage Span: c. 1920 – 1971/1973
  • Geographic Scope: Jharia Coalfield, Dhanbad.
  • Cause of Obsolescence: Nationalisation & Modern Public Sector Housing. The 1971–1973 Coking and Non-Coking Coal Nationalisation Acts transferred housing to BCCL/CIL, replacing private barrack allocations with institutional quarters.
  • Archival Provenance:
    1. The Calcutta Gazette (1935), Statutory Rules under the Jharia Mines Board of Health, Part II, Bylaw 6: "Construction of Dhowrahs". [Gazette Archive Link]
    2. Industrial Housing In India, documenting colliery housing and sanitation in the Bihar-Bengal coal belt. [Housing Survey Link]
    3. H. Coupland, Bengal District Gazetteers: Manbhum (1911), Ch. VI: Mines and Minerals (early colliery settlement notes). [Archive.org Link]

कमीन / কামিন (Kamīn)

>100 yr Old Colliery Labor / Gendered Occupational Argot
  • Definition: Colloquial term across the Jharia and Raniganj coalfields for female surface and underground coal loaders, operating within a family labor gang alongside a male coal cutter who cut the seam.
  • Active Usage Span: c. 1890 – 1939
  • Geographic Scope: Jharia & Raniganj Colliery Belts (Dhanbad & Burdwan).
  • Cause of Obsolescence: Legislative Prohibition of Female Underground Labor. Phased out by colonial mining regulations in 1929 and strictly enacted in 1939, excluding women from pithead coal-carrying operations.
  • Archival Provenance:
    1. Dr. Kuntala Lahiri-Dutt, "Kamins Building the Empire: Class, Caste, and Gender Interface in Indian Collieries" (2006). [Scholarly Text Link]

कमिया / Kamiya (Kamauti System)

>100 yr Old Agrarian Law / Unfree Labor
  • Definition: A landless agricultural laborer bound to a landlord through hereditary debt-bondage; compelled to provide manual field labor in return for subsistence grain, with no realistic capacity to extinguish the debt.
  • Active Usage Span: 19th Century – 1920 (Statutory ban)
  • Geographic Scope: Chota Nagpur & Southern Bihar Plains (Palamu, Gaya, Hazaribagh borders).
  • Cause of Obsolescence: Statutory Abolition. Declared unlawful under the Bihar and Orissa Kamiauti Agreements Act (1920), which voided long-term labor pledges.
  • Archival Provenance:
    1. The Abolition of Bonded Labour and Slavery in India: From 'Poor Law' to a Fundamental Right – the Gradual Changes of an Institution 1843–1990 (Section analyzing the Kamiya system and the 1920 Kamiauti Agreements Act). [ResearchGate PDF Link]
    2. H. Coupland, Bengal District Gazetteers: Manbhum (1911), Ch. VIII: Occupations and Landless Labor. [Archive.org Link]

चाकरान / চাকরান (Chākarān)

>200 yr Old Feudal Tenure / Rural Policing
  • Definition: Parcels of agricultural land granted rent-free or on nominal terms to village retainers, watchmen (chowkidars), and local public officers in lieu of fixed cash salaries.
  • Active Usage Span: Mughal Era – 1950s
  • Geographic Scope: Bengal Presidency & Manbhum District.
  • Cause of Obsolescence: Monetization of Rural Administration. The resumption of service lands by provincial governments and the subsequent 1950 Zamindari abolition replaced tenure-based compensation with direct cash salaries.
  • Archival Provenance:
    1. Durga Prasad, Dictionary of Law and Usage and Guide to Legal Translation (defining Chakran service lands). [Legal Dictionary Text Archive]
    2. H. Coupland, Bengal District Gazetteers: Manbhum (1911), Ch. XI: Land Revenue Administration ("Chaukidari Chakran lands"). [Archive.org Link]

पंचक / পাঞ্চক (Panchak)

>200 yr Old Agrarian Law / Land Revenue
  • Definition: A supplementary quit-rent or quit-tribute assessed on privileged holdings; in the Manbhum borderland, it specifically designated the nominal rent paid by subordinate Ghatwals to superior Zamindars.
  • Active Usage Span: Pre-Colonial (~1765) – 1950
  • Geographic Scope: Manbhum, Bankura, and Chota Nagpur frontier estates.
  • Cause of Obsolescence: Statutory Abolition of Feudal Intermediaries. The abolition of intermediate agrarian rent tiers under the Bihar Land Reforms Act (1950) unified all land payments into a uniform state assessment (lagaan).
  • Archival Provenance:
    1. Durga Prasad, Dictionary of Law and Usage and Guide to Legal Translation (defining extra assessments and cesses). [Legal Dictionary Text Archive]
    2. H. Coupland, Bengal District Gazetteers: Manbhum (1911), Ch. XI: Land Revenue Administration (Panchak rates on service tenures). [Archive.org Link]

How the words vanished

Five legal and administrative changes account for all seven words.

  1. 1920

    The Bihar and Orissa Kamiauti Agreements Act declared long-term labour pledges unlawful.

    Retired: Kamiya

  2. 1939

    Colonial mining regulations, phased in from 1929 and strictly enacted in 1939, excluded women from colliery coal-carrying work.

    Retired: Kamīn

  3. 1950

    The Bihar Land Reforms Act and the abolition of zamindari ended service-land and intermediate-rent tenures.

    Retired: Ghāṭwālī, Chākarān, Panchak

  4. 1956

    The States Reorganisation Act divided Manbhum between Purulia in West Bengal and Dhanbad in Bihar, now Jharkhand.

    Retired: Mānbhūm

  5. 1971–73

    The Coking and Non-Coking Coal Nationalisation Acts moved colliery housing to BCCL and CIL.

    Retired: Dhowrah

Method

The starting point was the 1911 Manbhum District Gazetteer. Its full text was analysed with Voyant Tools to surface recurring terms that are unfamiliar today. Candidate words were then traced in further printed and scholarly sources.

A word is included when it has a verifiable source, belongs to Manbhum, Chota Nagpur or the Jharia coalfield, and went out of use because a law, a border or an industry changed. Words that could not be traced to a source are left out.

Each entry gives a definition, a usage span, a geographic scope, the cause of obsolescence and its provenance. Entries are grouped into three eras: feudal and pre-colonial (more than 200 years), early industrial (more than 100 years) and late colonial to nationalisation (more than 50 years).
